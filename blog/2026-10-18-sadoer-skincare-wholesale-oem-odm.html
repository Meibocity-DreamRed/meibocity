Let's walk through the four questions you should ask any beauty supplier — and how SADOER answers them.

Question 1: Is This Brand Actually Reliable?

Reliability in the beauty trade is not about promises — it is about structure. Before you commit money to a new supplier, look for evidence that the company is built to sell internationally, not just to local walk-ins:

  • A serious product architecture. SADOER organizes its range into clear, professional categories: Daily Skincare (toners, serums, face masks, body lotions, face creams, cleansers, facial mists), Makeup, Face Masks, Serums, Salon Skincare (hydration, brightening, repair) and Salon Treatment Sets (anti-wrinkle, brightening, repair). A brand that has built salon-grade treatment sets alongside everyday care is targeting professional buyers, not just impulse shoppers.
  • A storefront built for export. The SADOER site runs in both English and Spanish, with product pages, bulk-ordering information and purchasing support written for international customers. That is a strong signal that the brand already serves overseas buyers and understands their expectations.
  • A defined wholesale program. The site explicitly states: "We support bulk orders across our full range of daily and salon skincare products. Wholesale customers enjoy dedicated wholesale pricing, bulk order discounts, and a stable, reliable supply." A supplier who names their wholesale program publicly has systems in place — not a "we'll figure it out" attitude.

The takeaway: look for category depth, export-facing infrastructure and a public wholesale commitment. SADOER checks all three — but you should still request their business registration and export records before your first large order.

Question 2: Is the Formula Safe for My Market?

Formula safety is where brands are born and destroyed. A compliance failure in your destination market does not just cost money — it can get your entire inventory seized. Here is what a responsible supplier should be able to provide, and the questions you should send to SADOER before sampling:

  1. Full ingredient transparency. Every SADOER product lists its key actives clearly — think hyaluronic acid, vitamin C, niacinamide, collagen, snail secretion filtrate and kojic acid across their serums, masks and treatment lines. Insist on a complete ingredient list for every SKU you plan to import.
  2. Documentation on request. Ask for the MSDS (Material Safety Data Sheet) and a COA (Certificate of Analysis) per batch. Any brand that ships internationally — especially to EU, US, Gulf or LATAM markets — should be able to produce these documents as a routine part of sampling.
  3. Destination-market labelling support. When you move from quote to order, confirm who handles destination-market labelling requirements and restricted-ingredient checks. Get this answer in writing before production, not after customs holds your shipment.

The takeaway: never accept "don't worry, the formula is fine" as an answer. Send the ingredient and documentation questions to SADOER before you approve a sample — the response time and the quality of the paperwork tells you more than any marketing page.

Question 3: What Are the MOQs — Small Orders and Big Orders?

This is where SADOER's dual structure shines. Most buyers come to the table with very different volumes, and a smart supplier prices each path differently:

  • Retail & wholesale (flexible, lower volume). SADOER's retail channel is designed for "personal shopping and bulk purchasing" with "flexible orders" — suitable for daily routines and first market tests. Beauty stores, small retailers and e-commerce sellers can start with modest quantities, test sell-through, and reorder as demand grows.
  • Bulk wholesale (dedicated pricing). For established retailers and group purchases, SADOER offers bulk ordering with wholesale pricing and order discounts across the full product range. The site's bulk-ordering section is explicit: "Ideal for beauty stores, retailers, and group purchases. Contact us for order quantities, pricing, and partnership details."
  • OEM/ODM private label (project-based MOQ). Custom work — your own formula or packaging, your brand name, gift set configurations — is a different conversation. Customization involves product development and packaging minimums, so the practical advice is to ask directly: start with ready-made formulations on a private label if your volumes are small, and move to fully custom development once you have reorder data.

The takeaway: the right MOQ question is not "how much is the minimum?" — it is "what does the minimum look like at each level?" A brand that offers both flexible retail wholesale and dedicated bulk pricing gives you room to grow without overcommitting on day one.

Question 4: Can I Approve Samples Before I Order?

Samples are the contract. SADOER's published collaboration process is exactly the workflow you want to see:

  1. Contact — tell the brand your purchasing needs (which products, which markets, which quantities).
  2. Get a quote and samples — the brand responds with pricing and sends samples for your review.
  3. Confirm your order — you approve the samples, the specs and the commercial terms.
  4. Production and shipping — the order is produced and shipped against the confirmed terms.

Any supplier who skips or rushes step two — or charges a suspiciously high "sample fee" before showing you real product — should be treated with caution. A serious brand treats the approved sample as a binding specification for the entire production run.

The Bottom Line for Smart Buyers

Whether you are a beauty store stocking shelves, an e-commerce brand launching your first private label, or a salon building a professional treatment menu, the checklist is the same:

  • Verify the brand — category depth, export-facing storefront, public wholesale program.
  • Demand formula documentation — full ingredients, MSDS, COA per batch, labelling support.
  • Match the MOQ to your stage — flexible retail wholesale to test, bulk pricing to scale, project-based MOQ for custom work.
  • Insist on samples before production — and treat the approved sample as the contract.
