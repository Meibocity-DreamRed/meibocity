How to Choose a Shampoo OEM/ODM Factory You Can Actually Trust

You have the brand name. You have the market. Maybe you even have the packaging design ready. The only thing standing between you and your first shelf-ready bottle of shampoo is one decision: which factory do you trust with your formula, your label and your money?

It is a fair fear. Every buyer in this industry has heard the horror stories — a "factory" that is really a trading desk, a formula that changes between sample and container, an MOQ that quietly doubles after you sign. Importers are right to ask hard questions before committing.

This guide is written from the perspective of Guangzhou Meiwan Plaza, the wholesale hub that sits at the centre of China's beauty manufacturing trade. We visit manufacturers every week, and we know exactly what separates reliable OEM/ODM partners from expensive lessons. Today we introduce one of them: Guangzhou Keli Biotechnology Co., Ltd., a dedicated OEM/ODM shampoo and hair care manufacturer — and we walk through the questions you should ask any factory, and how Keli answers them.

First: Make Sure the Factory Only Does OEM/ODM

If you are building your own brand, you do not want a supplier who competes with you on the same shelf. Retail-first factories will happily sell your private-label stock next to their own branded products, often at a price you cannot match.

Keli is an OEM/ODM-only manufacturer. The factory does not run a retail consumer brand that competes with its clients. Its entire business model is built around making your formula, your packaging and your brand — nothing else. From the first sample to full container production, the factory's job is to disappear behind your label.

Question 1: Is This Factory Actually Reliable?

The fastest way to check reliability is to look at hard facts, not marketing words. Here is what matters, and what Keli can show:

  • 22 years of manufacturing experience in hair care, with a dedicated R&D team of 20 years and a QC team of 20 years.
  • A 30,000 m² GMP-certified, dust-free facility that meets US and EU validation standards, with 16 automated production lines, 300 skilled technicians and up to 80 tons of capacity per 8-hour shift.
  • Five quality checkpoints before delivery — not one inspection at the end, but a series of gates through the production process.
  • Registrations and certifications, including GMPC, ISO 9001, ISO 22716 and FSC systems, plus EU CPNP registration and REACH compliance for European export.

These are not claims you have to take on faith. Keli publishes its certificates openly, and any buyer can request copies before placing an order.

The takeaway: ask for certificates, ask for factory photos, ask for existing export markets. A factory that has shipped to regulated markets — Europe, the Gulf, the Americas — has already been audited by someone with standards stricter than yours.

Question 2: Is the Formula Safe for My Market?

Formula safety is the question that keeps importers up at night, because a compliance failure in your market does not just cost money — it can kill the brand. Here is what a trustworthy factory must provide:

  1. Full ingredient transparency. You should receive a complete ingredient list and, on request, an MSDS for the formula. Keli provides MSDS documentation and can prepare COA (Certificate of Analysis) per batch.
  2. Destination-market compliance support. Keli already works with EU CPNP registration and REACH compliance, and holds export certificates covering markets including Saudi Arabia. If your target market has specific labelling or restricted-ingredient rules, the factory's R&D team adjusts the formula before sampling — not after you have received a customs penalty.
  3. Stable, documented formulations. The R&D team (20 years of hair care technology experience) keeps master formulas on file. Every batch is produced against the same written specification, and the 5-point QC process verifies consistency before shipment.

The takeaway: never accept "don't worry, it's fine" as a compliance answer. Ask for the document. Any serious OEM/ODM partner will send it before you pay a cent.

Question 3: What Is the Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ)?

MOQ anxiety is real, especially for first-time importers and e-commerce brands starting small. The answer should never be one rigid number shouted at you over email — it should be a conversation shaped by the product.

Keli's approach to MOQ is flexible and product-based:

  • Ready-stock formulas (their existing shampoo and hair mask lines, already developed and tested) can be private-labelled with low MOQs — ideal for market testing and small first orders.
  • Custom formulations — a new recipe developed for your brand, your scent, your active ingredients — naturally require a higher MOQ, because the R&D cost and raw-material minimums are real.
  • Volume breaks reward committed buyers: larger container orders bring better unit pricing, and the factory can hold raw materials against your forecast.

If your volumes are small today, the honest advice is to start with a ready-stock formula at a modest quantity, prove the product in your market, then scale into a custom formula once you have reorder data. A factory that pushes you straight into a huge minimum before you have sold a single bottle is a factory thinking about its own warehouse, not your success.

Question 4: Can I Approve Samples Before I Order?

This should not even be a question — but it is the one most worth confirming in writing. Yes: samples come first, production only after your approval.

The correct process, and the one Keli follows, looks like this:

  1. Brief — you send your product concept, target price band, packaging idea and destination market.
  2. Sample development — the R&D team prepares a formula sample (and, if useful, a packaging mock-up).
  3. Your review — you test texture, scent, lather, packaging and labelling against your expectations.
  4. Formula lock — after your approval, the master formula is frozen and production runs against that exact specification.
  5. Bulk production with QC gates — the 5-point inspection checks quantity, colour, scent, packaging integrity and labelling accuracy before shipment.

A factory's attitude to sampling tells you everything. If the supplier hesitates, charges a suspiciously high "sample fee", or promises the sample "will be basically the same" — walk away. Serious manufacturers treat the sample as a binding contract with the customer: what you approve is exactly what you receive.

The Bottom Line for Smart Buyers

Choosing a shampoo factory does not have to be a leap of faith. Do the same checks you would do with any serious supplier:

  • Verify the factory — years in business, scale, certifications, export records.
  • Demand formula documentation — ingredients, MSDS, destination-market compliance.
  • Agree MOQ around your plan — not their warehouse targets.
  • Insist on sample approval before bulk — and make sure the sample is the contract.
